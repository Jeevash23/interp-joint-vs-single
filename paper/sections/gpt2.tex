\section{Study 4: GPT-2 on indirect-object identification}\label{sec:gpt2}

Studies~1--3 use small networks and tasks with 27 inputs. Study~4 asks whether their results hold in a language model.
It was designed after the results of Studies~1--3 were known. The protocol, the predictions and the prompt lists were
hashed after the protocol was approved and before any model was run; a development phase followed, then a freeze of
the protocol, code and development settings, then the held-out confirmation. Two amendments and one
development-informed addendum were recorded before the data they concern (\cref{app:gpt2-history}).

\subsection{Design and predictions}\label{sec:gpt2-design}

\paragraph{Task and variables.} The task is indirect-object identification (IOI) in GPT-2
Small~\citep{wang2023ioi}, with the two variables of the IOI causal model of the MIB benchmark~\citep{mueller2025mib}:
the indirect object's position and its identity. For the results of Studies~1--3 to apply, every combination of
values must be an ordinary prompt whose answer follows from the two variables. So the \emph{position} variable $a$ is
which name repeats, with the first clause unchanged ($a=0$: the second name repeats, ABBA; $a=1$: the first repeats,
BABA), and the \emph{token} variable $b$ is which ordered pair of a task's three names $(n_0,n_1,n_2)$ fills the first
clause, $(n_b,n_{b+1})$. The answer is $n_{(a+b)\bmod 3}$, so it changes when either variable changes, as in the
additive tasks of Studies~1--3. The nuisance is the template. A task is a name triple and \NTemplates{} templates,
\NPrompts{} prompts of \NTokens{} tokens each, for example
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccll@{}}
\toprule
$a$ & $b$ & Prompt & Answer \\
\midrule
0 & 0 & When Laura and Bob went to the park, Bob gave a book to & Laura \\
1 & 0 & When Laura and Bob went to the park, Laura gave a book to & Bob \\
0 & 1 & When Bob and Tom went to the park, Tom gave a book to & Bob \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Names and template words come from fixed lists, every item one GPT-2 token; development and held-out tasks share no
name and no word. A task is used only if GPT-2 answers all \NPrompts{} prompts with a margin of at least
\CompetenceMargin{} logit, the competence margin of Studies~2 and~3. Development used the first \NTasksDev{}
development draws that passed, confirmation the first \NTasksConf{} held-out draws.

\paragraph{Sites and alignments.} The primary arm (arm a) aligns both variables at one site: the residual stream
after block $L$ at every position from the first name onward (\SiteDim{} numbers). This site carries everything the
variables affect on the way to the output; the final position alone carries only part of it, because later
name-mover heads read the name from the name positions. Per task it has five alignments: distributed alignment search
(DAS) with rank 1, 2 or 4 per variable and $P_AP_B=0$ by construction, trained on the single queries in which the
swapped variable changes; the conditional-mean contrasts of each variable, made orthogonal; and random orthogonal
subspaces of rank 2. Three secondary arms use the final position alone after the same block (arm a$'$), the MIB
assignment of position to heads 7.3, 7.9 and 8.6 and of token to head 8.10 at the final position (arm b), and
subspaces fitted one variable at a time at arm a's site, which need not be orthogonal (arm c). A further arm (arm d)
fits subspaces one variable at a time in Study~2's \TrnNetworks{} trained networks. The learning rate, number of DAS
steps and layer were chosen on development tasks by fixed rules that use single queries only: GPT-2 Small's were
learning rate \LrSmall, \StepsSmall{} steps and layer \LayerSmall{} (the latest of \CandSmall), with a tie band of
\TieBand{} set from measured numerical noise.

\paragraph{Queries, panels and costs.} Every alignment's census holds all \NSingle{} single queries (\NSinglePerVar{}
per variable) and all \NJoint{} joint queries. A query passes if the predicted name's logit exceeds the larger of the
other two names' logits by more than the tie band. The full-domain single panel holds the single queries in which the
swapped variable changes (\NFullA{} for position, \NFullB{} for token), and a test drawn from it alternates the two
variables; the joint panel holds the \NJointPanel{} joint queries in which both variables change. As in Studies~1--3,
a single query costs \CostSingle{} model calls and a joint query \CostJoint, and the detection probabilities of
\cref{sec:setting} are exact for a given task. The margin rule flags an alignment if any of its joint queries whose two
reduced single queries pass has $\sigma>1$, and a joint failure is irreducible if both of those single queries pass.
Intervals are 95\% bootstrap intervals over tasks (\NBoot{} resamples) or Clopper--Pearson intervals for shares.

\paragraph{Predictions.} Three predictions, carried over from Studies~1--3, were hashed before any model was run.
(1) Among alignments at one site that pass every single query, at most 10\% fail a joint query. (2) If any fails only
jointly, the margin rule flags at least half of them and at most 10\% of the others, with an area under the ROC curve
of at least 0.8. (3) Joint minus full-domain single detection at \Budget{} calls is negative, as it was in Study~2
(\TrnContrastOneTwenty). The protocol needs at least 10 alignments that pass every single query to score predictions 1
and 2. Further predictions concerned arms b, c and d. A bigger-model arm on GPT-2 Medium, added by an amendment before
any of its computation, carried predictions 1--3 to that model (\cref{sec:gpt2-medium}).

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:gpt2-results}

\GateWrongNamePctSmall\% of the failures GPT-2 preferred the wrong name on some prompt), so the GPT-2 Small
confirmation rests on \GatePassedSmall{} tasks, as the protocol prescribes when fewer than \NTasksConf{} pass. GPT-2
Medium's confirmation rests on \GatePassedMedium{} tasks (\cref{sec:gpt2-medium}). \Cref{tab:gpt2-onesite} gives the
one-site results for both models and \cref{tab:gpt2-predictions} scores every prediction.

\paragraph{Joint tests detect failures that the prespecified single tests miss.} At equal cost, a joint test of
\Budget{} model calls detects far more failing alignments than a single test drawn from the full-domain panel, by
\ContrastS{} in GPT-2 Small and \ContrastM{} in GPT-2 Medium (\cref{tab:gpt2-onesite}). The difference is positive at
every budget, from \ContrastSAtTwelve{} at 12 calls in GPT-2 Small. Prediction 3, carried over from Study~2, expected
the opposite sign, and does not hold, beyond sampling error, in either model.

\paragraph{The position subspace carries name information.} DAS of rank 2 and 4 fails none of the full-domain single
queries, the queries it is trained on, and rank 2 trained on half of them fails \SplitHeldOutS\% of the other half.
These queries change the swapped variable. The single position queries whose donor has the recipient's own position
but other names, which lie outside the full-domain panel, tell a different story: in GPT-2 Small rank-2 DAS fails
\UnchRTwoS\% of them and rank-4 DAS \UnchRFourS\%, and the two fail \JointRTwoS\% and \JointRFourS\% of the joint panel.
A joint query always brings a second donor's names into the recipient through the position subspace, so it meets this
leak on every draw; a full-domain position query changes the position at the same time, and the leak goes unseen.
Against single tests that draw from all single queries, which include the queries that reveal the leak, joint tests
still detect more failing alignments, but by much less: \ContrastAllS{} in GPT-2 Small and \ContrastAllM{} in GPT-2
Medium at \Budget{} calls, and \ContrastAllSAtTwelve{} in GPT-2 Small at 12 calls. This comparison was added after the
development results were seen and is descriptive.

\paragraph{Joint-only failures and the margin rule.} No GPT-2 Small alignment passes all \NSingle{} single queries;
rank-2 DAS, the closest, fails at least \MinSingleFailRTwoS{} on every task. In GPT-2 Medium \PerfectWordM{} alignments
pass every single query, all DAS of rank 2. That is fewer than the 10 the protocol needs, so predictions 1 and 2 are
not testable in either model. What happened in GPT-2 Medium is still informative: all \PerfectWordM{} fail joint
queries, between \MedJointFailMin{} and \MedJointFailMax{} of the \NJoint{} each, and the margin rule flags all
\PerfectWordM. In Study~2, by contrast, \TrnPerfectJointFail{} of \TrnPerfect{} such alignments failed jointly.

\paragraph{The final position and MIB's heads.} At the final position alone (arm a$'$), DAS fails \EndRateA\% of the
position and \EndRateB\% of the token queries of the full-domain panel, as predicted: the name that is said is read
from the name positions after this site. With MIB's assignment (arm b), \HeadsRateB\% of the token queries at head 8.10
fail, against \HeadsRateA\% of the position queries at heads 7.3, 7.9 and 8.6: under the counterfactual model, head
8.10 cannot change which name is said. Both tests then detect every arm-b alignment, so its contrast is zero, and
prediction 4, that it would exceed arm a's, does not hold (arm a's is larger by
\HeadsMinusOneAbs{}\ci{\HeadsMinusOneCILoAbs}{\HeadsMinusOneCIHiAbs}).

\paragraph{Subspaces fitted one variable at a time.} At arm a's site in GPT-2 Small, separately fitted subspaces
overlap little (median $\|P_AP_B\|$ \SepOverlapMedian, range \SepOverlapMin{} to \SepOverlapMax; prediction 5a holds),
and their joint failure rate falls rather than rises with the overlap (Spearman \SepSpearman, interval
\SepSpearmanCI; prediction 5b does not hold). In Study~2's \TrnNetworks{} networks (arm d), \DPerfect{} of \DAlign{}
separately fitted alignments pass every single query, and \DJointOnly{} of these fail a joint query (\DShare\%;
interval \DShareCI), against \TrnPerfectJointFail{} of \TrnPerfect{} (\TrnPerfectShare\%) for Study~2's orthogonal
alignments; the overlap separates the joint-only ones with an area under the ROC curve of \DAuroc{} (interval
\DAurocCI). Both predictions 6a and 6b hold beyond sampling error. The margin rule flags \DFlagged{} of the
\DJointOnly{} and \DFlaggedOther{} of the other single-passing alignments.
