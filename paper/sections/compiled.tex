\section{Study 1: compiled transformers}\label{sec:compiled}

\subsection{Programs, alignments and labels}

We compiled \CmpPrograms{} programs with Tracr \citep{lindner2023tracr}, \CmpPerFamily{} from each of two
families. A \emph{lookup} program returns $T[a,b]$ for a random $3\times3$ table $T$ with values 0--4 and
distinct rows and columns. An \emph{additive} program returns $A(a)+B(b)$, where $A$ and $B$ each map $\{0,1,2\}$
to three distinct values from 1 to 10. Each network reads the sequence \texttt{[BOS, a, b, n]}; attention copies
$a$ and $b$ to the last position, and the final MLP computes the output. The site is the residual stream at the
last position after the final attention layer. The edited coordinates are those that carry $a$ and $b$ (one-hot
for lookup, one scalar each for additive) and the three one-hot coordinates of the nuisance token, \CmpPoolLookup{}
dimensions for lookup and \CmpPoolAdditive{} for additive. The final MLP reads neither the nuisance coordinates nor
the categorical copies of $a$ and $b$ that additive networks also hold (its weights on them are exactly zero), so
the output is a function of the edited coordinates. A seeded random rotation of these coordinates is applied before
alignment and undone before the MLP, so that no alignment can use the compiler's axes; it is an exact change of
basis. Lookup networks have two layers, residual width \CmpResidLookup{} and \CmpParamsLookup{} parameters;
additive networks have three layers, width \CmpResidAdditive{} and \CmpParamsAdditive{} parameters. Every network
computes its program on all 27 inputs, and an independent NumPy implementation reproduces the compiled residual
stream to within \CmpNativeMax.

Each program received the six alignments in \cref{tab:compiled}. Two are valid by construction: the compiler's
own carrier coordinates, and the same with the nuisance contrasts added to $P_A$, which moves coordinates the MLP
never reads. Two take the conditional-mean contrasts of each variable, over all 27 inputs or over the 12 inputs
with $a,b\in\{0,1\}$. One is a distributed alignment search (DAS) rotation \citep{geiger2024das} trained on the 144
single interchanges among those 12 inputs that change the swapped variable. The last is random. An alignment is
labelled valid if every single edit sets the swapped variable's carrier to the donor's value and leaves the other
carrier unchanged, checked over all 729 recipient--donor pairs per variable without running any test. This gave
\CmpValid{} valid and \CmpInvalid{} invalid alignments. In the lookup family, two values of a variable fix only one
of the two directions that separate its three values, so both restricted alignments miss the direction that
distinguishes value 2. In the additive family each carrier is one-dimensional, two values determine it, and the
restricted alignments are exact; there the DAS starting point, the restricted mean contrast, was already exact and
the optimiser stopped before its first update. The compiler, full-mean and both restricted alignments of an
additive program therefore coincide, and the \CmpCandidates{} alignments contain \CmpDistinct{} distinct ones
(\CmpDistinctValid{} of the \CmpValid{} valid ones).

By construction, then, every alignment is either exact, so that its single edits land on their targets up to
coordinates the network ignores, or it misses a direction that single queries use: the restricted lookup alignments
miss the direction that separates value 2, and random subspaces miss the carriers. An alignment of the second kind
gives wrong outputs on the single queries that use the missing direction. No alignment can therefore fail only
jointly, and Study~1 cannot test the condition of \cref{cor:irreducible}. What its census can do is explain why the
frozen comparison of joint and single tests found no difference.
\citet{vaidyanathan2026mediators} give a second reason why compiled networks show little interaction between edits:
they are piecewise affine by construction, and the interaction term of activation patching vanishes to second order
where the downstream map is locally affine. That reason alone leaves joint-only failures possible: the network of
\cref{sec:theory} is piecewise affine too, and its joint queries fail through a unit that only joint edits switch
on. In Study~1 the construction rules them out.

The protocol, seeds and code of this study were frozen at \CmpFreezeTime, before the final programs were
generated. Every alignment was scored by tests drawn from eight panels at budgets of 12, 24, 60 and 120 model
calls:
\begin{itemize}
  \item restricted, full-domain and held-out single panels and the joint panel (\cref{sec:setting});
  \item complement resampling: the components outside $P_A$ and $P_B$ are taken from a random donor and the
  recipient's own output is predicted, the resampling ablation of causal scrubbing \citep{chan2022scrubbing};
  \item raw state distance: a jointly edited state must equal its target natural state;
  \item transported divergence: the difference between a jointly edited state and its target is added to a random
  natural state, whose output must not change, after \citet[Appendix A.3]{grant2026divergent};
  \item natural execution of the counterfactual inputs, which checks competence only.
\end{itemize}
The exhaustive census and the checks of \cref{sec:theory} are post hoc: they were added after the frozen results
and are exact computations on the saved networks and alignments.

\paragraph{The census confirms the construction.}
The composition identity holds to \CmpIdentityMax. Every single edit of every valid alignment lands on its target
apart from the nuisance coordinates the MLP ignores (largest coordinate difference \CmpValidOffMax), and every invalid
alignment fails single queries, its worst single edit missing its target by a coordinate difference of at least
\CmpInvalidOffMin. An alignment is valid exactly when it passes all 1,458 single queries (\CmpLabelsMatch{} of
\CmpCandidates), and none fails a joint query while passing every single query (\cref{tab:compiled}). The
\CmpIrreducible{} irreducible joint failures all belong to invalid alignments, which already fail single queries.

\paragraph{The frozen comparisons agree with the census within sampling error.}
Replaying each frozen panel's sampling rule \CmpSimReps{} times on the census, with queries shared by the alignments
of a program as in the frozen run, all \CmpSimInside{} frozen counts of accepted invalid alignments fall within the
central 95\% of the replayed counts. The \CmpSimTotal{} counts are seven panels at four budgets: every panel of
\cref{tab:compiled-panels} except natural execution, which does not depend on the alignment. \Cref{fig:compiled}
shows six of the seven; the seventh, raw state distance, accepted no invalid alignment at any budget. Each expected
count below is a range: its lower end counts near-tied queries as failures and its upper end as passes. At 120 calls
restricted singles were expected to accept \CmpRestrictedExpected{} invalid alignments and accepted
\CmpRestrictedAccepted. The
probability that any invalid alignment would survive 120 calls was \CmpSurviveSingle{} for full-domain singles,
\CmpSurviveJoint{} for joint queries, \CmpSurviveHeldout{} for held-out singles, \CmpSurviveFaith{} for complement
resampling and \CmpSurviveTransport{} for transported divergence. The frozen primary comparison, joint minus
full-domain single acceptance at 120 calls, was zero; given the census, any other result had probability at most
\CmpOtherResult. At 12 calls the joint test accepted \CmpJointTwelveObserved{} invalid alignments and full-domain
singles \CmpSingleTwelveObserved, against expected values of \CmpJointTwelveExpected{} and
\CmpSingleTwelveExpected; the difference is sampling variation.

\paragraph{Restricted tests miss restricted alignments.}
The \CmpRestrictedAlignments{} restricted lookup alignments pass all \CmpRestrictedQueries{} queries of the
restricted single panel, which for the DAS alignments are exactly the queries they were trained on, and fail
\CmpRateDasSingle\% (DAS; range \CmpRateDasSingleRange\%) and \CmpRateMeanSingle\% (mean contrasts;
\CmpRateMeanSingleRange\%) of full-domain single queries; minimal pairs over the full domain catch every one of them.
In program 10000, for example, the restricted mean alignment transfers only the contrast between $a=0$ and $a=1$:
moving $a=0$ from donor $(0,1,1)$ into recipient $(2,1,0)$ leaves the $A$-carrier at \ExCarrierAfter{} instead of
$(1,0,0)$, and the network returns 1 instead of $T[0,1]=2$. This is a known effect: an explanation fitted and checked
on part of the input range need not hold beyond it \citep{friedman2024illusions,rai2026datadriven}.
In the frozen tests, restricted singles accepted \CmpRestrictedAccepted{} of the \CmpInvalid{} invalid alignments (the
\CmpRestrictedAlignments{} restricted ones and one random alignment), while full-domain, held-out and joint queries,
complement resampling and transported divergence each rejected all \CmpInvalid{} and accepted all \CmpValid{} valid
ones. Raw state distance rejected the \CmpRawFalseRejected{} valid alignments that also move the nuisance
coordinates, a harmless divergence in the terms of \citet{grant2026divergent}; transported divergence accepted them.
