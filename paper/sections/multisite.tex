\section{Study 3: variables at different sites}\label{sec:multisite}

\subsection{Design and predictions}

When $A$ is aligned upstream of $B$, a joint query reduces to a single query only through $A$ (\cref{rem:sites}), so
the argument that made joint tests redundant in Studies~1 and~2 loses one of its two routes. Study~3 measures how joint
and single tests compare in this setting. It was designed after the results of Studies~1 and~2, and its two
predictions were recorded, with a hash of the protocol, at \MsPredictionsTime{} on 6 October 2026, before any of its
networks existed. First, the share of alignments that pass every single query but fail a joint query would exceed
Study~2's (\TrnPerfectJointFail{} of \TrnPerfect, \TrnPerfectShare\%). Second, joint minus single detection probability
at 120 calls would exceed Study~2's \TrnContrastOneTwenty. A prediction was to count as holding if the estimate exceeded Study~2's value, and as holding beyond sampling error if
its whole 95\% interval did.

The networks are trained by Study~2's frozen code, with the same settings and the same requirement that every input
be classified correctly with a logit margin of at least 1, on 25 new tables per family and with the same four
architectures (\MsNetworks{} networks). In the MLPs, $A$ is aligned at the first hidden
layer and $B$ at the second, each after its ReLU. In the transformers, $A$ is aligned at the residual stream of
position~1, which holds the token $a$, before attention, and $B$ at the residual stream of the last position after
attention, Study~2's site. Each network receives five alignments: DAS with rank 1, 2 or 4 at each site, the two
projectors fitted separately on the 486 single interchanges of their variable that change it, with Study~2's
settings; the conditional-mean contrasts at each site; and random subspaces of rank 2. We took the census of all
1,458 single and 19,683 joint queries of every alignment, with the panels and costs of Study~2. A joint failure is
\emph{unexplained} if both single queries it relates to, $(r'',t)$ and $(r,s)$, pass. At the transformer's $A$ site
there are only three distinct natural states, one per value of $a$, so an $A$-edit whose subspace contains their
differences lands on its target exactly, and every joint query of such an alignment is a single $B$-query. The
protocol, code and final seeds were frozen at \MsFreezeTime, after development on four tables per family
(Appendix~\ref{app:multisite}).

\paragraph{Networks and alignments.}
Of the \MsNetworks{} networks, \MsCompetent{} met this requirement; one lookup-task transformer of width 64 missed it
at all three initialisations and is excluded, as the protocol prescribes, which leaves \MsAlignments{} alignments. The DAS
alignments fail on average \MsRateA\% of the single queries of $A$, \MsRateB\% of those of $B$ and \MsRateJoint\% of
the joint panel (\cref{tab:multisite}): $B$, aligned nearer the output, is the weaker variable in \MsMechBMore{} of the
\MsDas{} DAS alignments. At the transformer's $A$ site every $A$-edit lands on its target in \MsLandTransformer{} of the
\MsDasTransformer{} DAS alignments and in every mean-contrast alignment; no MLP $A$-edit does.

\paragraph{Prediction 1: joint-only failures.}
Of the \MsAlignments{} alignments, \MsPerfect{} pass every single query, \MsPerfectTransformer{} in transformers and
\MsPerfectMlp{} in MLPs, all of them on additive tasks. Of these, \MsPerfectJointFail{} fail a joint query, all in MLPs, on
\MsJointOnlyMinFailures{} to \MsJointOnlyMaxFailures{} of the 19,683 joint queries (at most \MsJointOnlyMaxPanelShare\%
of the joint panel, all of these failures unexplained); a joint test of 120 calls would detect one of them with
probability \MsJointOnlyDetect\% on average. The share, \MsPerfectShare\% (95\% interval \MsPerfectCI\%), is above
Study~2's, so the prediction held, but the interval contains Study~2's value, so it did not hold beyond sampling
error. None of the \MsPerfectTransformer{} transformer alignments fails a joint query, including the
\MsPerfectTransformerLand{} whose $A$-edits all land, for which \cref{rem:sites} rules it out.

\paragraph{Prediction 2: detection.}
At 120 calls, joint minus single detection probability is \MsContrast, with a 95\% interval of \MsContrastCI{} from
10,000 resamples of networks as in Study~2. It is above Study~2's \TrnContrastOneTwenty, so the
prediction held beyond sampling error. It is positive in every cell of \cref{tab:multisite}, and also at equal
numbers of queries (\MsEqual) and with cached natural states (\MsCached). After a single test of 120 calls, 120 more calls
detect a further \MsAddonJoint\% of the alignments if spent on joint queries and \MsAddonSingle\% if spent on single
queries. Post hoc, most of the gain comes from how the budget is spent rather than from failures that only joint
queries find. Every joint query exercises $B$, the weaker variable, while an alternating single test spends half its
budget on $A$. Where every $A$-edit lands (\MsMechLanding{} transformer alignments), each joint query is a single
$B$-query, the joint failure rate equals the $B$ failure rate exactly, and the contrast is still
\MsMechLandingContrast. Against single tests that spend all 120 calls on whichever variable fails more often, which a
tester would have to know in advance, joint minus single detection is \MsMechWorse. Unexplained failures make up
\MsUnexplained\% of the joint-panel failures of the DAS alignments (\MsUnexplainedTransformer\% in transformers,
\MsUnexplainedMlp\% in MLPs), against an irreducible share of \TrnIrrShare\% in Study~2.

\paragraph{The signed rule out of sample.}
The scores $\sigma$ and $\rho$ and the rule $\sigma>1$ were fixed on Study~2 before Study~3's protocol was recorded.
Study~3's frozen code computes both scores, with the one term that \cref{rem:sites} leaves,
$\sigma=-\nabla\mu_c\cdot u/\mu_B$ for the $B$-query $(r'',t)$, where $u=(w-z^B_{r''})(I-P_B)$ and $w$ is the state
that the $A$-edit produces at $B$'s site; it also computes their query-level areas under the ROC curve by kind. The
alignment-level ranking and the breakdown by architecture below were coded after Study~3's results were known and
apply the rule unchanged (Appendix~\ref{app:multisite} gives the times). Ranking the \MsPerfect{} alignments that
pass every single query by their largest $\sigma$ over all joint queries puts the \MsPerfectJointFail{} joint-only
ones at positions \MsSrRanksSigma{} (area under the
ROC curve \MsSrAlignAucSigma), and the rule flags exactly these \MsSrFlagged{}; $\rho$ ranks them at positions
\MsSrRanksRho. Query by query, for the joint-panel queries of the DAS alignments whose two single queries pass, $\sigma$
separates the unexplained failures with an area under the ROC curve of \MsSrAucSigmaTs{} and \MsSrAucSigmaTl{} in
transformers of width 16 and 64 and \MsSrAucSigmaMs{} and \MsSrAucSigmaMl{} in MLPs of width 16 and 64; $\rho$ gives
\MsSrAucRhoTs, \MsSrAucRhoTl, \MsSrAucRhoMs{} and \MsSrAucRhoMl, and the distance of the $A$-edit from its target
\MsSrAucDistTs, \MsSrAucDistTl, \MsSrAucDistMs{} and \MsSrAucDistMl. In the MLPs the network is linear after $B$'s
site, so the first-order change is exact unless the runner-up class changes; the transformers are the harder test,
and there $\rho$ does better than $\sigma$ query by query, as it did over all of Study~2's alignments.
