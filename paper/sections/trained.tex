\section{Study 2: trained networks}\label{sec:trained}

\subsection{Networks and alignments}

Study~2 uses the same two task families, with 25 new tables per family, and four architectures for each table:
transformers with model width 16 or 64 and MLPs with hidden width 16 or 64, \TrnNetworks{} networks in all. Each
transformer reads \texttt{[BOS, a, b, n]} with learned token and position embeddings and has one causal attention
layer with two heads and pre-LayerNorm, an MLP four times as wide as the model, and a final LayerNorm; its site is
the residual stream at the last position after attention, as in Study~1. Each MLP reads the one-hot input and has
two hidden ReLU layers; its site is the first hidden layer after the ReLU. Networks were trained in double precision
on all 27 inputs to classify the table value, and had to classify every input correctly with a logit margin of at
least 1 (Appendix~\ref{app:trained}). All \TrnCompetent{} networks met this standard, \TrnFirstAttempt{} at the first
of up to three initialisations.

Each network received six orthogonal alignments: DAS with rank 1, 2 or 4 per variable, trained on the 972 single
interchanges of the full-domain single panel; DAS with rank 2 trained only on the 144 queries of the restricted
single panel; the conditional-mean contrasts of $A$ and $B$, made orthogonal by symmetric orthogonalisation; and
random subspaces of rank 2. A full-domain DAS alignment is therefore scored on the queries it was trained on, and
passing every single query measures how well it fits them; a post hoc held-out split below asks what this does to
the comparison. For every alignment we took the census of all 1,458
single and 19,683 joint queries. The protocol, code and seeds were frozen at \TrnFreezeTime, after development on
four tables per family and before any final network was trained; the protocol records the development results.
Unlike Study~1, there is no validity label independent of the tests, and the study measures how single and joint
tests relate to each other.

\paragraph{Linear alignments in trained networks are approximate, and the condition holds on the whole joint panel.}
The full-domain DAS alignments fail on average \TrnSingleRate\% of full-domain single queries and \TrnJointRate\% of
joint-panel queries (\cref{tab:trained}); joint failure rates track single failure rates closely and are on average
a little lower (\cref{fig:trained}a). Of the \TrnCandidates{} alignments, \TrnPerfect{} pass every single query:
\TrnPerfectMlp{} in MLPs and \TrnPerfectTransformer{} in transformers, all of the latter on additive tasks. No
alignment of a lookup-task transformer passes every single query; the best of a network's alignments still fails
\TrnLookupTransformerBest\% of full-domain single queries (median over networks). What follows about alignments that
pass every single query therefore rests mainly on MLPs, on \TrnPerfectTransformer{} alignments for transformers, and
on none for lookup-task transformers. No single edit that changes a variable lands on its target: post hoc, the smallest distance between such
an edit and its target, relative to the typical distance between natural states, is \TrnLandMinAll{} over all
alignments and \TrnLandMinPerfect{} over the \TrnPerfect{} that pass every single query. Both single edits of a
joint-panel query change their variable, so the condition of \cref{cor:irreducible} holds for every joint-panel
query of these \TrnPerfect{} alignments.

\paragraph{Joint-only failures are rare and sparse.}
Even so, only \TrnPerfectJointFailWord{} of the \TrnPerfect{} fail a joint query: \TrnJointOnlyDasCount{} DAS alignments of
rank 2 and one mean-contrast alignment, \TrnJointOnlyMlp{} of them in MLPs and \TrnJointOnlyTransformer{} in a
transformer. Post hoc, each fails between \TrnJointOnlyMinFailures{} and
\TrnJointOnlyMaxFailures{} of the 19,683 joint queries. Of the seven, \TrnJointOnlyPanelCount{} fail queries of the
joint panel, at most \TrnJointOnlyMaxPanelShare\% of them, and \TrnJointOnlyOutsidePanel{} fails only joint queries
in which one variable keeps its value. A joint test of 120 calls would detect one of the seven with probability \TrnJointOnlyDetect\% on
average and at most \TrnJointOnlyDetectMax\%. How far their single edits move from their targets does not single them
out: the median relative distance is \TrnJointOnlyOffRange{} for these seven and \TrnPerfectOffRange{} for the
alignments that fail no joint query.

\paragraph{Few joint failures are irreducible.}
Across the full-domain DAS alignments, \TrnIrrShare\% of joint-panel failures are irreducible; all but
\TrnIrrInPerfect{} of the \TrnIrrChecked{} occur in alignments that also fail single queries. Per joint query the
irreducible rate is \TrnIrrRate\%, against a single failure rate of \TrnSingleRate\%. Post hoc, each irreducible
failure has both single edits off their targets, the nearer at a relative distance of at least \TrnSmallestOff. In
the prespecified exploratory analysis, the larger of the two distances separated irreducible failures from passing
joint queries slightly better than the smaller one (area under the ROC curve \TrnAucMax{} against \TrnAucMin),
contrary to the protocol's expectation; the size of the divergence predicts these failures only weakly.

\paragraph{The signed margin score picks out the joint-only failures (post hoc).}
For the \TrnMbQueries{} joint-panel queries of the full-domain DAS alignments whose two reduced single queries pass,
\TrnMbIrreducible{} of them irreducible failures, we computed the first-order changes of \cref{prop:margin}
(\cref{eq:split} holds to \TrnMbIdentity). The unsigned score $\rho$ was the one we specified first; the signed score
$\sigma$ was added to the same analysis before either had been computed, both were computed in one run on these data,
and we chose $\sigma$ as the main score afterwards, because \cref{prop:margin} says only drops can cause failures.
Ranking the \TrnPerfect{} alignments that pass every single query by their largest $\sigma$ over all 19,683 joint
queries puts the \TrnPerfectJointFailWord{} joint-only ones at positions \TrnMbRanksSigned{} (area under the ROC curve
\TrnMbAlignAucSigned), and the rule $\sigma>1$ flags \TrnMbRuleFlagged{} of the \TrnPerfect{} alignments, all of them
joint-only; it misses \TrnMbMissed, the only transformer among the seven. Ranked by $\rho$, the seven are at positions
\TrnMbRanks{} (\TrnMbAlignAuc). Query by query the order reverses: as a score for the failures, $\sigma$ has an area
under the ROC curve of \TrnMbAucSigned{} and $\rho$ of \TrnMbAuc, against \TrnAucMin{} and \TrnAucMax{} for the two
distances. The first-order change has the sign of the actual change in \TrnMbSign\% of cases but in only
\TrnMbSignIrr\% for the failures, and a failure whose first-order change points the wrong way in either ordering gets
a negative $\sigma$; $\rho$ ignores the sign. The rule $\sigma>1$ is precise but incomplete: of the queries it flags,
\TrnMbRuleTP{} fail and \TrnMbRuleFP{} pass, and it misses \TrnMbRuleFN{} failures. For the MLPs, the Lipschitz bound
of \cref{prop:margin}(b) shows without running them that \TrnMbLipCertified{} of the \TrnMbLipQueries{} joint-panel
queries (\TrnMbLipShare\%) pass, and none of them fails.

\paragraph{At the prespecified costs, joint tests detect fewer failing alignments than single tests.}
The useful comparison is what a joint test adds to a single one. After a full-domain single test of 120 calls, a
further 120 calls spent on joint queries detect a further \TrnAddonJoint\% of the \TrnDas{} full-domain DAS
alignments, and the same calls spent on more single queries a further \TrnAddonSingle\% (post hoc). Head to head,
with three model calls per single query and four per joint query, the prespecified convention, joint tests detect
fewer alignments than full-domain single tests at each budget in \cref{fig:trained}b: joint minus single detection
probability at 120 calls is \TrnContrastOneTwenty, with a 95\% interval of \TrnContrastOneTwentyCI{} from 10,000
resamples of networks within each architecture and family, \TrnContrastTwelve{} at 12 calls, and negative in every
cell of \cref{tab:trained}. Post hoc, with the same resampling, it is \TrnEqualQueries{} \TrnEqualQueriesCI{} at equal
numbers of queries (30 of each), positive in \TrnEqualPositiveCells{} of the eight cells, and \TrnCached{}
\TrnCachedCI{} if natural states are cached so that every query costs one call (120 of each), positive in
\TrnCachedPositiveCells{} cells and within sampling error of zero. Minimal-pair
singles detect fewer failing alignments than full-domain singles (\TrnDetectMinimalOneTwenty{} against
\TrnDetectSingleOneTwenty{} at 120 calls), since a minimal pair never changes the other variable and so cannot reveal
an edit that disturbs it. Restricted singles detect \TrnDetectRestrictedOneTwenty. Every detection curve is capped at
\TrnCeiling, because \TrnNeverFail{} of the \TrnDas{} alignments pass every query of the full-domain single and joint
panels, which contain the queries of the other panels.

\paragraph{Held-out single queries (post hoc, exploratory).}
Because a full-domain DAS alignment is trained on the full-domain single panel, a single test scores it on its own
training data, while every joint query is new to it. To see what this does to the comparison, we refitted the
full-domain DAS alignments of every network with Study~2's settings on a random half of those queries (243 per
variable) and took their census. The refitted alignments fail on average \TrnHoTrainRate\% of the single queries they
were trained on, \TrnHoHeldRate\% of the held-out ones and \TrnHoJointRate\% of the joint panel. Held-out single
queries are thus slightly harder to pass, and against them joint tests fall a little further behind: at 120 calls,
joint minus held-out single detection is \TrnHoContrast{} (95\% interval \TrnHoContrastCI; \TrnHoTrainContrast{}
against the training half) with three and four calls per query, \TrnHoEqual{} at equal numbers of queries and
\TrnHoCached{} with cached natural states. After a held-out single test of 120 calls, 120 more calls detect a further
\TrnHoAddonJoint\% of the alignments with joint queries and \TrnHoAddonSingle\% with held-out single queries. Scoring
single tests on their training queries therefore did not hide an advantage of joint tests. Of the \TrnHoPassChanged{}
refitted alignments that pass every single query in which the swapped variable changes, \TrnHoPassBothFailJoint{} fail
a joint query.

\paragraph{Restricted fitting.}
DAS alignments trained only on the restricted inputs fail \TrnCovRestricted\% of restricted single queries and
\TrnCovFull\% of full-domain ones. Of the \TrnCovCandidates, \TrnCovPassRestricted{} pass every restricted query, and
all \TrnCovFailFull{} fail some full-domain query.
